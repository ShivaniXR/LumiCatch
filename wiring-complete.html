Neon-Net Wiring

Both subsystems on one page: the MPU-6050 on I2C and the haptic motor on D9. This supersedes the Phase 2 card, which covered the motor alone.

Three things that bite

The MPU is 3.3 V only. On 5 V its pull-ups drag SDA and SCL to 5 V and you risk the whole I2C bus.

Transistor pinout varies by manufacturer. Check your own part. A transistor that warms up means base and collector are swapped: cut power.

Diode band goes to the supply side. Backwards it conducts continuously and shorts the rail through the motor.

03 The shared rail, and the fault it already caused

The motor and the IMU are on the same 3V3 rail. The motor pulls roughly 90 mA the instant it starts, and that dip is enough to reset an MPU-6050.

This is not hypothetical. During Phase 1 the IMU reset itself when the breadboard was swung, and the failure was silent: asleep, the MPU still acknowledges on I2C and simply returns zeros for every reading. It looks exactly like a dead sensor rather than a power problem.

The firmware now detects this and re-wakes the sensor automatically, printing IMU had reset to sleep. That is a safety net, not a fix. If the message appears when the motor fires, do one of these:

FixCostWhen to use it
Move motor + to 5VNothing, rewire one leadBest fix. The transistor already switches the low side and handles 5 V comfortably, so nothing else changes. The motor stops loading the rail the IMU depends on
Decoupling capacitorAbout ₹5100 µF electrolytic plus 100 nF ceramic across the MPU's VCC and GND. Buffers the dip locally
Separate suppliesA second power bankOverkill here, and more to carry on the net

The build was tested on 3V3 and worked, so do not churn a working assembly for its own sake. But if the IMU starts dropping out once the motor is firing during real play, the 5 V move is the first thing to try and it costs nothing.

04 Mounting it on the net

  • The IMU must be rigid. Tape or glue it hard against the handle near the hoop. A sensor that can wobble smears the acceleration signal, and the trajectory model reads slope and curvature, so smearing degrades it directly.
  • Secure every jumper. This is a breadboard being swung through the air. Push each pin fully home, use the shortest wires you have, and tape the bundle so strain lands on tape rather than pins.
  • Solder the MPU's four wires if you can. It removes this whole class of failure before filming.
  • Motor against the handle where your top hand grips, in direct contact so the buzz carries into your palm.
  • Power bank near the base of the handle. It doubles as a counterweight for the hoop.
  • Re-measure the swing threshold after mounting. Near the hoop the lever arm is longer, so the same swing reads higher than it did on the bench. Expect to raise SWING_THRESHOLD_G above 2.2.

05 Before you power up

  • MPU VCC is on 3V3, not 5 V.
  • SDA and SCL are not swapped.
  • Diode band is on the supply side.
  • Transistor flat face toward you, legs in three separate columns.
  • No bare motor lead touching another column.
  • Both subsystems share one GND.

Expected on boot: one short confirmation buzz, then LumiCatch net ready in Console > Serial Monitor. Swing it and you get Swing peak: 5.50.

Measured on this build: PWM floor 55, motor coast-down 80 to 100 ms, IMU at rest 0.87 g, walking to 1.08 g, a real swing peaking at 5.50 g. Every threshold in the firmware is set from those numbers rather than from theory.
